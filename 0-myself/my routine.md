## 🌙 Phase 0: Pre-Launch (Evening Before, ~10:00 PM or earlier)

This part is just setting things up so tomorrow doesn't start with decisions. You're closing today's tabs in your mind and laying out tools for the morning.

### Tomorrow's Note
Open your notes app, go to tomorrow's date page. If it doesn't exist, create it and paste your daily template—`## Evening Review`, `## Today’s Top 3`, and a capture section. Don't fill anything in. The point is that the page is there, waiting.

### Physical Setup
Take two minutes and do three things:
1. Lay out every piece of clothing you'll wear tomorrow on a chair—socks included.
2. Fill a glass of water and put it on your nightstand.
3. Plug your phone in across the room, or in another room entirely. Not near the bed.

After that, you pause once and think: *"Today is done, tomorrow is staged."* Then go do whatever you do before bed, and when you turn the lights off, that's the lid on the day.

## 🌅 Phase 1: The Launch (5:45 AM – 7:45 AM)

This block handles your body's needs and gets your head clear before any external input.

### Wake Window (5:45 AM – 6:30 AM)
The alarm goes off—soft, gradual. You put your feet on the floor. No staying in bed trying to negotiate awake-ness. First action: reach for the glass and drink half of it. That's it.

Now make your bed. Takes under two minutes. You're just pulling sheets and duvet straight, placing pillows. While you're doing it, you think: *"This is the first thing I finished today."* That thought isn't poetry; it's just a fact, and facts stack up.

### Bathroom Reset
You go to the bathroom. Splash water on your face. Then brush your teeth. While standing there brushing, do ten slow calf raises—just because motion feels better than standing still. After brushing, scrape your tongue if that's part of your routine, and moisturize your face.

Now stop for a second. Ask yourself: *"Energy out of ten?"* If it's five or above, do the five-minute primer. If it's under five, go straight to a warm shower and let yourself ease in.

### Five-Minute Primer (only if energy ≥5)
*Minute 1:* Box breathing. Four counts in, hold four, out four, hold four. Do four cycles. You're just breathing.

*Minutes 2-3:* Forward fold, halfway lift, downward dog. Do that flow three times. Match your exhale to the folding part; it makes the stretch easier.

*Minute 4:* Seated spinal twist, thirty seconds each side.

*Minute 5:* Child's pose. Stay there and mentally repeat your key phrase once: *"Capture, connect, execute calmly."*

### Shower
Shower as usual. At the end, turn the water cool for thirty seconds. Breathe through it. You step out feeling more alert. That's the only reason to do it.

### Breakfast
Eat something real—oats, eggs, fruit, yogurt. Before you sit down, put your phone in another room, facedown. While eating, put your fork down between bites. Chew slowly. Count to twenty sometimes. If you're alone, read a page of a book that has nothing to do with university, or just sit with the quiet.

While you're eating, you're not consuming information. You're just eating. That's the whole rule.

### Top 3 + Daily Note
When the plate is empty, go to your desk and open the daily note you pre-created last night. Under `## Today’s Top 3`, write: *"What three things—if I get them done today—will actually move my projects forward?"* Write them. At least one must be small enough to finish in a single 25-minute Pomodoro.

Now link each task to its larger project note. Example:
- `- [ ] Resolve confusion about covariance matrices [[Statistics for ML]]`
- `- [ ] Complete problem set 4 [[PHYS 201]]`
- `- [ ] Read ML paper intro [[ML Mastery Pathway]]`

Under the list, write your if/then line. The one you said you liked:

> *"If I feel the urge to scroll during deep work, I will put my phone in my bag and breathe three times."*

That's your fallback script. Then close the note.

### Deploy
At 7:40, a silent alarm vibrates. Check your bag: laptop, charger, notebook, water bottle, phone. As you walk out, think: *"I'm on campus mode now."*

## 🎓 Phase 2: The Deep Work Zone (Campus, from arrival until you leave)

This is the stretch where your phone stays away and your job is to be present in lectures and capture what you don't understand.

### Arrival
The moment you step onto campus, turn on Do Not Disturb—not vibrate, full DND. Put your phone inside a specific pouch in your bag, not a pocket. Walk into the lecture hall. Pick a seat in the first three rows, off-center. Put your water bottle on the desk. Open your notebook to the course page (like `[[PHYS 201]]`).

Before the lecture starts, look at the `#struggle-concept` tags you saved from last time. Write one question at the top: *"💡 Curious about: [something specific from last class]."*

### In-Class Note Margin
Take notes with a wide right margin. Whenever something doesn't make sense—when you feel that mismatch—draw a question mark in the margin and write a short tag: `? Lag operator intuition`. Don't try to resolve it now. Just mark it and continue.

At the top of every hour, or when the instructor changes topics, take three sips of water. Set a silent alarm if that helps.

### Post-Class Two-Minute Capture
The instant class ends, don't touch your phone. Open your daily note, start a two-minute timer, and dump:

- *Key takeaways: 1. ... 2. ...*
- *Confusions: 1. concept X because ... 2. How does A relate to B?*
- *Links: reminds me of [[Previous Topic]]*

On each confusion, add `#struggle-concept` and link to a hub note like `[[Physics Struggle Hub]]`. You are not solving them now. You are moving them from your head to paper.

When the timer ends, close the note, take three deep breaths, stand up and stretch your arms overhead. Then move to the next class. That's the reset.

## 🏡 Phase 3: Recovery & Re-Entry (Post-Campus, ~5:00 PM onward)

You're home. The goal is to detach from campus identity and restore some energy before evening work.

### Arrival
Place your bag on its designated hook or shelf. Say it, even just internally: *"Recovery time. I'm off the clock."* Then go wash your hands and splash cool water on your face. It's a physical full stop.

### Meal
Make your meal—this can be lunch/dinner combined. Before you eat anything else, eat at least a cup of non-starchy vegetables. Finish them completely before starting the rest of the plate. That sequence blunts the glucose spike that would otherwise make you crash later.

Halfway through the meal, put your fork down, take a breath, and ask: *"Hunger, one to ten?"* Then continue.

### Recovery Block
Stand up after eating and rate your fatigue: high (≥7), moderate (4–6), or low (≤3).

- **High fatigue:** Set an alarm for 25 minutes. Lie down with a light blanket. Nap. When the alarm sounds, sit up, drink water, do five jumping jacks or a downward dog to pull yourself out of sleep inertia.
- **Moderate fatigue:** Lie on your back, palms up, and do a guided Yoga Nidra (Non-Sleep Deep Rest) for 20–30 minutes. Follow the voice. When you finish, stand up, stretch, and say: *"I'm restored."*
- **Low fatigue:** Skip the nap/NSDR. Use the time for something restful—music, a short walk, nothing demanding.

## 🌆 Phase 4: The Modular Evening (Execution & Growth)

Now you shift into work mode, but you choose the intensity based on a quick honest scan.

### Capacity Check
Open a small note containing your version matrix:

| Version | Time & Energy | Academic Pomodoros | Upskill Pomodoros | Wind Down Start |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| **High (A)** | Home early, energy high | 4–5 | 2 | 10:30 PM |
| **Standard (B)** | Home later, moderate energy | 3 | 1 | 11:30 PM |
| **Fallback (C)** | Burnt out or very late | 1–2 | skip | 10:00 PM |

Check the actual time, your subjective energy, and how many `#struggle-concept` tags you collected today. Then write your version in your daily note: *"🌜 Evening Version: B."* That's your container. No guilt.

### Academic Block
Before you start, clear your desk. Only your laptop/notebook and the sticky note with your Top 3 stay. Start the 25-minute Pomodoro timer. Take one deep breath and think: *"Focus block. No task switching."*

**First Pomodoro:** Go to your daily note, find the most foundational `#struggle-concept` from today. Spend 25 minutes on it alone. Look it up, compare sources, write out the explanation in your own words. When it clicks, create a permanent note: `[[Eigenvector Intuition]]`. In that note, write your own explanation, add a diagram if you can, and link back to the lecture note where it first appeared. Then remove `#struggle-concept` and replace it with `#resolved` and the date.

**Subsequent Pomodoros:** Work on your Top 3 assignments. Use the timer strictly. After completing a task, draw a checkbox in your daily note and write a quick line: *"Problem set done. Got the dimensionality part."*

### Between Pomodoros
When the timer rings, stand up immediately. Look at something 20 feet away for a minute. Do ten air squats. Sip water. Jot in your daily note margin: *"Pomo 1: energy 7, resolved covariance."* Then reset for the next.

### Upskill Block (if your version includes it)
After academic Pomodoros are finished, change something physical—move to a different chair, shift a lamp, or switch to a different desktop. Open your Growth workspace and your skill pathway note like `[[ML Mastery Pathway]]`. Pick the next tiny sub-skill and set a 25-minute timer.

Study for 23 minutes. In the final 2 minutes, write a capture:
- *Key insight: [what shifted your thinking]*
- *Open question: [what's still fuzzy]*
- *Link to atomic note: [[Gradient Boosting vs AdaBoost]]*

Then close the Growth workspace entirely. Write one sentence in your daily note's `## Growth Log`: *"Today I implemented a decision tree and understood Gini impurity."*

After that, say: *"Guilt-free time. I earned it."* Then do something purely for enjoyment—hobbies, a call, a show, whatever. No academic or growth overlay.

## 🌜 Phase 5: The Shutdown Sequence (Wind Down)

This is a script you run every night to close your mental loops and prepare for sleep.

### Brain Dump
About 30 minutes before your digital sunset (10:00 PM for High, 11:00 PM for Standard), open the `## Evening Review` section of your daily note.

First, look at your Top 3. Write a brief note for each: *"Task 1 done—finished the problem set and went deep on the math,"* or *"Task 2 started, carried to tomorrow."* Then add a subheading: *"🧠 Loose ends."* List everything still in your head—emails, worries, random to-dos. Once they're written down, pick one priority for tomorrow and add it to tomorrow's daily note (which you created the night before). This transfer is what lets your brain relax for the night.

### Physical Release
Do a few static stretches: neck side bends, shoulder rolls, doorway chest stretch, seated forward fold, supine twist, legs up the wall for a couple minutes. While stretching, deliberately relax your jaw and eyes.

### Digital Sunset
Plug your phone in across the room—or in another room. Use a standalone alarm if needed. Now spend the remaining time until bed doing something screen-free: read a physical book, journal with no agenda, listen to a sleep story on a speaker. You might open a `[[Gratitude Log]]` and write three small good moments from the day. Not forced, just honest.

### Bedtime
The room is cool and dark. Lie on your back. Breathe in four counts, hold seven, exhale eight. Do that four times. On the last exhale, think: *"The day is closed."*

And that's the whole thing. The same routine, just without the "starting gun" and similar lines. Every step is still there, every habit linked to the next. The quotes are simple reminders you'd actually use. The headers keep the shape. This is your day, built to run.

### 🧹 Late Morning: The Prep Sprint (10:00 AM – 11:00 AM, one hour only)

You set a timer for 60 minutes. This hour has one job: execute the Open Day Prep List. No perfecting. The mantra:

> *“Good enough now is better than perfect never. Tomorrow’s success depends on my energy, not my bag’s precision.”*

You move through it briskly. After each tiny task, you check it off in your daily note. That little mark gives you momentum.

At the end of the hour, if you didn’t finish, you take 5 minutes to decide: is anything left a genuine dealbreaker? If not, you close the list anyway. The leftover becomes one line for later or for tomorrow morning. Then you do a symbolic close: close your closet door on the laid-out clothes, zip your bag and put it by the door. Think:

> *“Prep done. The rest of today is free.”*

---

### 🎯 Midday: Optional Light Review (1 Pomodoro, only if desire is genuine)

This is not mandatory. You sit with yourself and ask: *“Do I actually want to review anything for tomorrow?”* If the answer is a clear yes—maybe you want to recall talking points for a project showcase or draft questions for professors—you do exactly one 25-minute Pomodoro.

During that Pomodoro:
- You open a note called `[[Open Day Queries]]`.
- You jot down questions, ideas to mention, or topics you’d like to hear about.
- You do not study. You do not try to learn anything new. You’re just mapping your enthusiasm.

When the timer rings, you close the note and think: *“I have enough. What matters tomorrow is being curious, not rehearsed.”*

If the answer to “do I want to review?” is no or maybe-not-really, you skip this entirely. No guilt. You trust your preparation from the week.

---

### 🌿 Afternoon: Pure Leisure & Gentle Social Time

From noon onward, the day is yours. You’ve done the prep; you’ve earned the space. You lean into your Friday Anchor.

If you’re meeting someone, you go. If you’re alone, you cook something that takes time, or watch a film, or read. The only rule is you do it fully:

> *“I am here. Not half in tomorrow.”*

If anxious thoughts about the open day intrude, you use the if/then. Take out your phone or a paper and write the worry down exactly. Then ask: can I solve this now? Most of the time, the answer is no. You then tell yourself: *“Tomorrow-me will handle this. Today-me is resting.”*

---

### 🌜 Friday Night Shutdown (Earlier Than Usual)

Since the open day likely starts early, you shift the entire Wind Down forward.

#### 8:30 PM: Evening Review & Brain Dump
Open Friday’s daily note. Under `## Evening Review`:

- Mark the prep list as done.
- Write a few lines on how the day felt: *“Prep hour was calm. Afternoon with a novel. Nervous about tomorrow but ready.”*
- Create the `🧠 Loose ends` list. Any lingering worries go here.
- From those, pick one single thing that you can do in 5 minutes tomorrow morning for peace of mind. Write it in Saturday’s daily note if you’ve created it.

Then open the `[[Open Day Queries]]` note you made (or a new one) and read your questions once. This is not studying; it’s priming. Think: *“Tomorrow I’m not being tested; I’m exploring.”*

#### 9:00 PM: Physical Release
Full slow stretch: neck, shoulders, spine, hips. Legs up the wall for 5 minutes. While stretching, do a body scan: deliberately release jaw, brow, and hands.

#### 9:30 PM: Digital Sunset
Phone across the room. Pick a screen-free wind-down: a novel, journaling, listening to music. You can write a brief gratitude log: three small things from the freedays.

#### 10:00 PM: In Bed
Lights low. Four-seven-eight breathing, four cycles. On the last exhale, think:

> *“The freedays did their work. I am rested. I am ready to be curious.”*

---

That’s your weekend operating system in full, friend. Wednesday night opens the gate. Thursday restores and reminds you why you love learning. Friday balances gentle prep with deep leisure so you step into the open day not as a tired student clawing for survival, but as someone clear-eyed, calm, and genuinely curious. Every tiny action in these three phases is a stitch in that fabric.
